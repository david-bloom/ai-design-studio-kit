## What this records

After Handoff 007's plates (`projects/project-crux/visual/007-working-mode-challenger.md`, "The Blue Hour Cabinet") were committed, David and the CD ran a focused color study on that direction alone, outside this repo. This file is the written record of that study's outcome, so the decision is durable in GitHub rather than only living in the external canvas session.

**Scope:** color register only. This did not touch the cabinet/drawer structural system, the cyanotype/photogram medium, the scoring-anchor shapes, or the interaction language — those remain exactly as specified in `007-working-mode-challenger.md`.

## The locked register — grade 4a

- **Surround:** warm paper-white (not the original dark "Blue Hour" surround).
- **Chrome:** dark.
- **Center field:** graduates cool Prussian → warm lamplight.
- **Drawers:** sand.
- Blue signature retained. Full anchor discipline retained and unchanged:
  - **Blue won-flower** — the earned/available-point anchor.
  - **Fired-clay lost-filament** — the commonly-lost-point anchor.
  - **Cyan** — action/interactive color (thread), never doubles as either scoring anchor.

## What this resolved

**The fatigue doubt.** The open question was whether a fully dark field would tire a student across a 20-minute working session. Grade 4a answers it: a concentrated blue center on a warm-white frame outperforms a full dark field for sustained sessions — the white surround gives the eye a rest zone the all-dark version didn't have, while the center field keeps the blue signature that makes the direction recognizable.

## What was tried and rejected

- **Green** — rejected. Loses the register's drama without actually warming the experience; a flat trade, not an improvement.
- **Red** — rejected. Reads as "error" to a student, and collides directly with the fired-clay lost-point anchor — an unacceptable anchor-meaning conflict per the charter's one-meaning-per-anchor hard constraint.
