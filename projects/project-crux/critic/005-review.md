# Critic Review 005 — Six-Option Visual/UX Set

> **Status: advisory only — not final, not approved, not a Done decision.**  
> Reviewed by Grok (xAI) per Handoff Packet 005 against `projects/project-crux/visual/004-directions.md` and the charter’s Visual Design + UX Done Decider items. David is the sole Done Decider.

## Scope and method

- Source: `004-directions.md` (frozen consolidation of Options 1–6).
- Lens: heuristic evaluation against charter Done Decider (Visual Design + UX tracks) **and** scenario walkthroughs as Learner A (senior, strong executive function, density-seeking) and Learner B (sophomore, mild ADHD, low-decision / short-session needs).
- Hard-constraint check applied to every option.
- Option 6 evaluated under the explicit, temporary suspension of the no-character rule (controlled experiment only).
- Findings severity-tagged: **Blocking** (would prevent a clean Done call without change), **Notable** (material risk or gap that should be addressed before or during further iteration), **Minor** (polish / secondary).
- Comparative read is input to David’s decision, not a selection or ranking that carries authority.

## Shared observations across the set

**Positive (applies to most options)**  
- Scoring-anchor systems are consistently exclusive and non-overlapping with action colors.  
- Help-not-vend assistance ladders appear in the stronger interactive options.  
- Laptop-first, real AP content (equations, graphs, extended response) is taken seriously; no obvious phone-first or gamified patterns.  
- Progressive disclosure is at least gestured at in every option (Learner B defaults lighter; Learner A can pull more).

**Cross-cutting gaps**  
- **Notable — Overview / “place” mode is under-specified.** The charter’s UX Done Decider requires the overview/navigation (“place”) mode and the focused single-topic (“working”) mode to be *concretely expressed*, not just named. Almost all six options live almost entirely inside the Open Hand / working-mode experience for a single question. Spatial-memory navigation of the curriculum as terrain, mastery legibility, and recommendation-as-destination receive little to no concrete expression. This is a set-level gap, not unique to one option.  
- **Notable — Transfer vs. spectacle risk** is acknowledged in several options’ own “Risk to test” notes but is not yet stress-tested against real 10–20 min session completion for Learner B.  
- No option introduces streaks, leaderboards, paywall nags, or parent surfaces — hard constraints hold.

## Per-option findings

### Option 1 — Draw the Door: Influence Lab

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: yes — cool, precise, non-pandering register. Not worksheet-like; not a re-skin of Brilliant or Photomath.  
- Scoring anchors (yellow closed loop / guava open loop): exclusive and well-motivated.  
- Dual-profile support: strong — simulation telemetry optional for A; B sees graph + one prompt + one control.  
- Anti-reference survival: good.  
- Open Hand expression: excellent and specific — face-up sample with live scoring mechanics, prediction-before-action, transfer instance.  
- 10–20 min session for B: plausible if the two-phase interaction is tightly scoped; risk of over-complexity if both phases + writing are required in one short session.  
- Progressive disclosure: present and intentional.

**Hard constraints**  
All listed shared constraints appear satisfied. Purple interaction color never scores; anchors exclusive; help ladder ordered and transparent.

**Scenario walkthrough**  
- **Learner A**: Can dive deep into residual patterns, exact values, and rubric codes. Density is available without feeling gated. Risk of staying in the simulation longer than productive if the “predict then move” gate is weak.  
- **Learner B**: The single-control default and prediction prompt are protective. Potential stuck points: (1) understanding that Phase 1 (moving the line) does *not* change *r* while Phase 2 (moving the point) does — the distinction is pedagogically valuable but cognitively heavy for a short session; (2) the transfer writing task after the sample may feel like a second full problem rather than a short continuation. Visible momentum is present via live residual / slope updates, but the writing step can break flow if the response surface is too open.

**Severity summary**  
- **Notable**: Phase 1 vs Phase 2 distinction is powerful teaching but may overload a 10–15 min Learner B session unless the sample is aggressively time-boxed.  
- **Minor**: Accessibility controls are thorough on paper; real keyboard + screen-reader testing still required.

### Option 2 — Soft Landing

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: strongest in the set for restorative, low-anxiety feel. Translates wellness language without becoming clinical or gamified.  
- Scoring anchors (deep-harbor pebble / apricot ripple): exclusive and distinct.  
- Dual-profile: card stack + “whole hand” / mini-outline for A; sequential single-card for B.  
- Anti-reference: good — far from Kaplan / streak-heavy apps.  
- Open Hand: “Turn the hand” reveal of scoring mechanics is concrete and usable.  
- 10–20 min for B: the “three useful moves” session promise and single-card focus are well-matched to short sessions.  
- Progressive disclosure: explicit and elegant.

**Hard constraints**  
Satisfied. Action color (sky blue) never scores.

**Scenario notes (lighter pass)**  
Learner B benefits from the enforced sequential calm. Learner A may feel restricted if the backward path and “whole hand” view are not immediately discoverable or keyboard-accessible. Risk noted in the option itself is real: sequential calm can hide relationships A wants to scan.

**Severity summary**  
- **Notable**: Overview/place mode still absent; the card stack is a working-mode device.  
- **Minor**: Horizontal/vertical card transitions need careful motion and focus management so they do not feel like a carousel.

### Option 3 — Pocket Universe

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: high-wonder, jewel-saturated register. Optimistic and curious rather than calm; still non-pandering if the simulation stays academic.  
- Scoring anchors (acid-leaf hex lens / coral severed bridge): exclusive and metaphorically strong.  
- Dual-profile: one highlighted control for B; comparison / CI / raw data unlock for A.  
- Anti-reference: good if spectacle is controlled.  
- Open Hand: scoring lenses *inside* the model is a strong, specific expression.  
- 10–20 min for B: high risk — continuous manipulation + scale shifts can consume the entire session before writing begins.  
- Progressive disclosure: present but the field itself is dense by default.

**Hard constraints**  
Satisfied on paper. Cyan manipulation color correctly separated from scoring.

**Scenario notes**  
- **Learner A**: Will love the zoomable field and comparison mode.  
- **Learner B**: Easy to get lost or over-stimulated. The option’s own risk note (“simulation could consume attention without improving transfer”) is the central concern. Prediction-before-motion and explanation-after are necessary but may not be enough if the visual field remains seductive.

**Severity summary**  
- **Blocking (for short-session B completion)**: without aggressive time-boxing or a forced “close simulation → write” gate, Learner B is likely to leave a 15-minute session without a complete written response.  
- **Notable**: Place/overview mode still missing; the “universe” is topic-scale, not curriculum-scale.

### Option 4 — Lantern Fold

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: warm, contemplative, evening-reading-room register. Strong safe-haven quality.  
- Scoring anchors (gold square seal / raspberry dog-ear): exclusive, tactile, memorable.  
- Dual-profile: fifth technical leaf for A; B never needs it.  
- Anti-reference: excellent — paper-cut / folio metaphor is far from worksheet or gamified edtech.  
- Open Hand: scoring leaf that keeps the graph aligned across the fold is concrete spatial memory.  
- 10–20 min for B: accordion pacing can support short sessions if the number of leaves is fixed and low.  
- Progressive disclosure: good.

**Hard constraints**  
Satisfied. Persimmon interactive color never scores.

**Scenario notes**  
Learner B gets reliable page geography and low decision load. Learner A can open the technical leaf. Horizontal accordion + trackpad / magnification risk is real and correctly flagged in the option; vertical fallback is required for accessibility and practicality.

**Severity summary**  
- **Notable**: Horizontal interaction must have robust keyboard + button alternatives or it becomes a blocker for some users.  
- **Minor**: Place mode still absent.

### Option 5 — Say It Bright (original, text-only)

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: hottest, most outward register. Empowering and clear; risk of reading as loud or slightly didactic rather than calm.  
- Scoring anchors (ultramarine double underline / aubergine strike-gap): exclusive, typographically precise, non-decorative.  
- Dual-profile: sticky mini-index + Director’s view for A; single scene + one action for B.  
- Anti-reference: good — graphic-novel clarity without becoming a comic or a worksheet.  
- Open Hand: scene-by-scene reveal of “what the scorer can see” is a strong, usable expression.  
- 10–20 min for B: well-matched — one commitment per scene, visible progress through the sequence.  
- Progressive disclosure: explicit.

**Hard constraints**  
Satisfied. Punch Red action color never scores.

**Scenario notes**  
Learner B benefits from the forced single-focus scenes. Learner A can jump via the mini-index. Large type + saturated color can feel patronizing if real AP content is not given enough visual weight relative to the display type; the option’s own risk note is accurate.

**Severity summary**  
- **Notable**: Emotional temperature is high; for students who already find the current product “bleh,” this may either cut through or feel like another high-energy educational surface. Needs real student reaction.  
- **Minor**: Vertical space consumption on laptop height must be prototyped with actual Biology diagrams and Statistics tables.

### Option 6 — Say It Bright: Three Guide Registers (character experiment)

**Constraint frame**  
No-character rule is *suspended for this option only*, by David’s explicit instruction, as a controlled experiment. Evaluated as a real candidate, not an automatic violation and not exempt from scrutiny.

**Heuristic vs. Done Decider**  
- Calm / optimistic / empowering: inherits Option 5’s hot register; characters add emotional layering (wonder / peer / companion). Whether this lands as empowering or as soft distraction depends on the specific register and on whether the character ever competes with content for attention.  
- Scoring anchors: correctly kept separate from character art (ultramarine / aubergine never used on the guides).  
- Dual-profile / progressive disclosure: same structure as Option 5; Hide Guide preference is a necessary safety valve.  
- Anti-reference: characters increase risk of reading as “cutesy edtech” if any register drifts young or decorative. Guardrails (no applause, no correctness reaction, no collectibles, limited rail footprint) are strong and necessary.  
- Open Hand: identical to Option 5; characters only frame, never answer.  
- 10–20 min for B: same scene structure; character presence is additional visual load that must not increase decision count.

**Hard constraints**  
Satisfied under the suspension. Characters do not become scoring anchors, rewards, or answer-vendors.

**Scenario walkthrough**  
- **Learner A**: Can ignore or hide the guide and use Director’s view. Character is mostly noise unless the specific register feels peer-like (Moxie Quill is the strongest candidate for A).  
- **Learner B**: Potential benefit — a calm or curious presence at transition moments may reduce the “bleh / cold interface” reaction and provide a low-stakes anchor for attention. Potential harm — any motion, speech lozenge, or silhouette that pulls the eye away from the experiment diagram or the response box increases cognitive load. Bramblekin’s grounded stillness is the lowest-risk; Fizzbit’s elastic energy is the highest-risk for distraction or age-skew.

**Honest read on whether character presence is doing real work**  
- The experiment is well-designed: identical scene, identical support line, only the illustration register varies. That isolation is valuable.  
- Characters *can* address the “bleh” problem if they make the scoring truth feel less bureaucratic and more like a shared observation. The guardrails (hide preference, no reaction to correctness, framing-only appearance, never on the graded response) are the difference between useful presence and gimmick.  
- Risk of drift is real: once characters exist, future iterations may expand their role (idle animation, reaction, “mascot for the subject”). The current spec resists that expansion; any Done consideration for this option should treat the guardrails as hard, not soft.  
- Comparative to Option 5 (text-only baseline): Option 6 is not automatically better. It is a higher-variance bet. If student reaction data later shows the character versions outperform the text-only baseline on engagement *without* harming transfer or seriousness, the suspension was productive. If they read as decoration or age-inappropriate, the original no-character instinct was correct. The Critic cannot settle that; only real use can.

**Severity summary**  
- **Notable**: Character presence is a stylistic and brand risk even under suspension; Fizzbit carries the highest age-skew / busyness risk, Moxie Quill the highest snark risk, Bramblekin the highest nostalgia / decoration risk.  
- **Minor**: Alt-text and Reduce Motion handling are specified; still need verification.

## Comparative read (input to David, not a selection)

Motivation context: real students found the current live product’s design/UX “bleh.” The question is which directions are most likely to move that reaction while still serving both cognitive profiles and protecting the help-not-vend, non-gamified, clean-room constraints.

**Cluster most likely to change the “bleh” reaction**  
1. **Option 1 (Influence Lab)** — highest pedagogical specificity and kinetic payoff tied directly to rubric teaching. The live residual / influence interaction is the clearest differentiator from both worksheet edtech and passive “pretty” interfaces. Risk is cognitive load for Learner B.  
2. **Option 5 (Say It Bright text-only)** and **Option 2 (Soft Landing)** — both give strong, low-decision sequential structure and clear emotional temperature (hot-clear vs. cool-restorative). Either could feel like a deliberate, human-designed experience rather than a generic tool.  
3. **Option 4 (Lantern Fold)** — strong spatial memory and tactile warmth; less kinetic teaching power than Option 1 but higher safe-haven quality.

**Higher-variance / higher-risk cluster**  
- **Option 3 (Pocket Universe)** — maximum wonder and continuous simulation; highest chance of spectacle without transfer if gates are weak.  
- **Option 6 (character experiment)** — maximum emotional differentiation from a cold interface; highest chance of reading as gimmick or age-skew if any register slips. Valuable as a controlled probe, not yet as a default.

**Under-represented across the entire set**  
Concrete expression of the curriculum-scale “place” / overview mode. Any direction that advances should be paired with at least one concrete proposal for how the same visual system makes the broader curriculum navigable as terrain and mastery legible, or the UX Done Decider item remains unmet.
